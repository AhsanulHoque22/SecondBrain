# Post 1 — Introduction to my Second Brain

I just finished 6 graduate exams in 7 weeks — one every Wednesday, 10:30 AM, no exceptions. I didn't survive that on willpower. I survived it on a system I built and call my "Second Brain." This series is about what's actually in it.

Here's the short version.

It's an Obsidian vault, version-controlled in git, run by Claude Code. Not a note-taking app I dump things into — an active collaborator that reads my course slides, tracks my confidence per topic, schedules spaced repetition, and rewrites my daily plan every night based on what I actually did that day (not what I hoped to do).

A few things that made it work:

→ **Past-papers first.** Before touching a single topic, the system finds the exam pattern — what's actually been asked, which years, how often. Studying without that is studying blind.

→ **A "wiki" layer, not raw notes.** Every topic gets read from the source material exactly once, compiled into a dense one-screen page, and never re-read. Everything after that — quizzes, recall questions, revision — hits the compiled page, not the original PDF.

→ **A hard boundary for my startup.** I'm also building Livora. The rule was simple: 2 hours a day, zero in the last 2 days before any exam. The system enforced that boundary better than I would have on my own.

→ **A mobile front-end.** A Telegram bot let me update my log, mark a topic done, or queue an overnight build task for Livora from my phone between prayers or on a commute.

None of this was pre-built. It grew one protocol at a time — a repeated task became a documented routine, then automation, then something the system improves on its own.
